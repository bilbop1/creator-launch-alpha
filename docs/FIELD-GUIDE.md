# From links to a channel

This is a menu and an operating loop, not a fixed niche or a promise that another creator's results will transfer. Short and long videos, animation, commentary, documentary and other formats can all use it. The creator decides the scope, audience and budget.

## 1. Choose the opportunity

Start with one or more reference URLs, a topic, or a viewer you want to serve. Ask: who is the viewer, what experience are they looking for, what would make them click, and why would they return? Capture the answer and constraints in `brief.md`. If you only have one reference, seek adjacent channels and comparable misses before choosing a format.

Look beyond the obvious subject. A creator, celebrity, game, product, world event, rule change, impossible bargain, social situation, time ladder or everyday wish can be the hook. A person can be central to the premise, appear briefly in the opening, or be a supporting visual. Compare those options on immediate recognition, clarity, audience fit, storytelling potential, production effort and available evidence. No famous name is necessary for every story, and nobody's enthusiasm or permission should be assumed.

## 2. Build a reference set

For each channel, record a stable identity and selection criteria. Include typical uploads, high performers and weak performers, with observation dates and source links. Compare similar formats and ages where possible. Identify what you actually watched, what you inferred from metadata and what was unavailable. Avoid treating a single viral video as a typical result.

For each representative video, record the opening image and spoken hook, premise, sequence of beats, payoff, length, pacing, visual and audio devices, title and thumbnail. Capture visual observations by watching, not by inferring shots from a title or transcript. The beat sheet in `references.md` is a starting point.

## 3. Reverse-engineer and choose

Write testable statements: feature, proposed direction, comparison, sample, size and uncertainty. Use within-channel performance, not just raw view counts. Compare across independent channels and split the sample when feasible. Pre-register decisions where the evidence will determine a major commitment; save the plan before collecting the decisive data. See `RESEARCH.md`. A small set can guide an experiment, but cannot establish a general rule.

Translate observations into principles that can be executed independently: for example, "the first image makes the tradeoff legible" or "each beat changes the stakes." Make a creator-owned visual and narrative identity. Generate contrasting concepts; check each against references for overlap, feasibility, hook and a satisfying ending. A word match is a warning to investigate, not a verdict on the idea.

## 4. Make one complete pilot

Pick one concept and define the viewer promise, title and opening image. Draft narration or dialogue, a beat-by-beat visual plan, asset list, audio plan, caption strategy and a production budget. Choose tools and voice only after hearing or testing suitable options. Use original, licensed or otherwise appropriately authorized assets. Track their provenance.

Build a rough cut, watch and listen to the entire thing, and revise where the action, meaning or sound is unclear. Test at phone size if it is destined for phones. Mechanical checks can find missing audio, bad dimensions or clipping, but they do not judge whether a joke lands or a story is understandable. Keep the project editable and note what is still missing.

## 5. Decide and learn

Before launch, review title, thumbnail, claims, rights, platform rules and the complete final export. The owner chooses whether and when to publish. Separate a local export, an uploaded draft, accepted platform processing and a public video in reports. After publishing, use the channel's own analytics and a predeclared comparison window; avoid interpreting early volatility as a causal verdict. Keep useful findings, failures and the next test in the project log.

For creator-focused satire or caricature, use the real subject and framing deliberately: a visible hypothetical or joke, not an invented claim presented as fact or implied endorsement. Review current platform policies and applicable rights for the actual treatment, especially realistic synthetic likeness or voice, sensitive allegations, and commercial branding. Color or a changed name alone does not establish clearance; a creator's clipping program does not automatically authorize every unrelated use. Get specific professional advice where a real decision requires it.
