# Build and review a pilot

Choose a production method for the story, not the other way around. A channel might use motion graphics, live action, a narrated montage, hand-drawn animation, 3D, or another approach. Study the reference's visible grammar, then design a distinct identity and make or license the needed assets. This alpha does not install a renderer, include character art, or choose a voice provider for you.

## A practical build packet

1. **Premise and packaging:** write the audience promise, a working title and the first image. Develop a few title/opening variants against the same viewer promise. If a famous creator is an ingredient, compare using them as the central premise, a quick hook or a cameo, and a version without them. Choose for comprehension and fit, not name recognition alone.
2. **Script and beat sheet:** for each beat write what the audience learns, what changes, the visual action, dialogue/narration and transition. Test whether the story makes sense with the sound off and whether the narration makes sense without the picture; decide where each test is relevant to your format.
3. **Asset plan:** enumerate every character, scene, prop, recording, font, effect and music cue; record source and permitted use. Pick voice and sound by listening to auditions within your budget. Avoid baking secrets or provider-specific voice IDs into shared files.
4. **Build:** create a small representative test before producing the full cut. Iterate on staging, character readability, movement and sound; save editable source and any parameters another agent needs to reproduce the result.
5. **Review:** render the complete cut, then watch it with audio at its intended size. Check opening clarity, causality, continuity, captions, mix, pacing, ending and whether it fulfills the title. Record timestamps, defects and taste calls. After changes, review the changed render; a script, successful build or sampled still cannot establish a good final video.
6. **Delivery:** inspect the final file's technical metadata with `launchkit probe` if ffprobe is installed. Record dimensions, duration, streams, final path and full playback review. Check platform requirements and account settings only when needed and with appropriate authorization. The creator decides the final upload and release.

## Improve the system

If the same defect appears repeatedly, fix the shared template or generator and recheck a representative export. Keep reusable components generic and independently usable. Track actual results and their limitations after release: source references, production choices, measured outcomes, costs and the next hypothesis. A passed technical check is evidence of that check only, not proof of channel success.
